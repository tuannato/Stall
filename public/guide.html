Stall

how a stall works

One link. Two ways to sell.

A stall is a page that reads the eCash chain for one seller. It holds no key and signs nothing: your wallet signs, Stall shows. Two things can be on it — listings, which are Agora offers your wallet made, and quotes, which are prices you published for items you deliver yourself.

Listings — sell a token on Agora

An Agora listing is a token locked in a covenant with an asking price. Anyone can buy it from any wallet that speaks Agora; Stall gathers yours under one link.

  1. 01

    List a token in Cashtab In Cashtab, open a token you hold and list it on Agora with the price you want. That listing is a transaction from your address.

  2. 02

    Paste your address at stall.cash The one Cashtab shows on its Receive screen. Your stall opens at stall.cash/s/ + your address. That link is yours to share. Nothing is sent to us; what this browser keeps — the stall you open by default and the ones you pinned — stays in your own browser.

  3. 03

    Name it, then share it Studio → Name & look publishes a name, a tagline and a look in one small record your wallet signs. Studio → Share gives you the link, a QR and a poster.

What a buyer sees

The price the covenant asks.

Every row shows the smallest amount that buys from your listing, read from the chain. Stall never computes a price of its own.

Buying happens in Cashtab.

“See offers in Cashtab” opens the token’s order book. Cashtab opens this token’s offers and preselects the cheapest one the viewer can afford, which may belong to another seller.

Cashtab’s book is not your stall.

Cashtab does not name the seller. Its prices are per token, and sometimes in fiat — not the minimum take on this card.

Nothing here signs.

Cashtab builds and signs the payment, not this site. Stall never holds a key, never sees one, and cannot reverse a purchase.

Quotes — name a price, get paid directly

A quote is a small record your wallet signs onto the chain: your price for a token your wallet minted, in USD or XEC. Buyers tap Pay and their wallet pays your address. You deliver the item yourself.

Seller — set a price

  1. 01

    Open your stall → Studio → Items & prices A token you list already has a row. A token you minted but never listed does not: press “Describe a token” and paste its id.

  2. 02

    Describe & price Write what buyers should know, type the figure, pick USD or XEC. Your own asking figure, published as you write it. Nothing here converts it.

  3. 03

    Sign with Cashtab One small transaction. The quote shows under Quotes once the network has it; payments show under Activity, public like the rest.

Buyer — pay

  1. 01

    Open the seller’s link, tap Quotes Or scan the QR on their poster or stream — it opens the same item.

  2. 02

    Tap Pay and read the figure The XEC your wallet signs, the seller’s quote it comes from, and the rate it was converted at. A rate older than two minutes is fetched again before anything opens.

  3. 03

    Sign in your wallet You pay the seller directly. No escrow. No token is sent to you — the seller delivers off-chain.

The rules of a quote

Yours.

Your price per whole token, in USD or XEC, signed by your stall’s wallet. The record stays on chain until you publish over it.

Your token.

The editor refuses a new quote on a token another wallet minted — it would borrow that token’s name and picture — warns when it cannot tell who minted one, refuses the price field on a token this page does not carry, and keeps a quote already published. On a row, “Token names this stall” says the token’s own record points at this stall — by the minter’s claim, or by paying the minted supply here — and never who owns its name; “Token minted by another wallet” is the warning a buyer sees.

Direct.

Payment goes to your address. No escrow, no middleman. The page shows paid, never delivered — delivery is the seller’s promise, as with any seller you reach through a chat. Trust the seller as you would there; this page cannot vouch for them. Stall is not a party to the sale: it holds no account and no funds, takes no cut of a payment, and cannot reverse or mediate one.

Final.

A payment is final once the network has it. This page cannot reverse one, and neither can the seller’s wallet on your behalf.

Converted once.

A USD quote becomes XEC at the moment of paying, at CoinGecko’s rate as this page read it; the seller receives XEC and checks it against their own reckoning. An XEC quote never converts. A seller may publish a tolerance of 1–100%: their record says a payment short by up to that much still counts as paid in full. The page prints the tolerance and never judges whether a payment covered a quote — the seller decides. With none published it says “The seller has not stated a tolerance.”

Whole units.

Whole tokens only. Nothing under 5.46 XEC — the network would not relay it.

Public.

Quotes, payments, the memo that names the item and the address a payment was sent from are chain data anyone can read, on the Activity tab of the seller’s page. The memo is the payer’s claim, not a receipt.

Not shown.

Firma, fCHF, fEUR, XECX and tokens named after well-known coins are not painted as a listing or a quote. The list is a snapshot this page ships, and it can fall behind.
